# Operating Models

Five starting profiles. Each one sets the **nouns** the chief of staff uses, the **ladder** work moves along, and the **extra rules** specific to that kind of business.

Pick the closest during the interview, then tune it. The profile is a starting point, never a cage. If the person's real business straddles two — a therapist who also runs workshops, a nonprofit with an earned-revenue arm — take the primary and note the second in `Business Context.md` as a secondary line of work.

**The nouns matter more than anything else here.** A nonprofit director does not have a "pipeline"; she has funders and programs. If the map says "deals" to her, she stops trusting it in the first ten seconds.

---

## A. Nonprofit / mission organization

**Nouns:** funders, grants, programs, board, donors, volunteers, partners
Not: leads, deals, customers, pipeline

**Ladder:** prospect → LOI or application submitted → under review → awarded → active grant → report due → renewal or close-out

**Money looks like:** grant awards, individual and major gifts, event revenue, earned income, reimbursements owed to you

**Who matters:** program officers, board chair and committee chairs, major donors, fiscal sponsor, partner organizations, key staff

**Extra rule — dated:** a **reporting, compliance or filing deadline goes to Band A**, above everything else, including money conversations. A missed report can cost the next grant. Surface these earliest and by date, not by urgency.

**Watch for:** board asks that arrive by email and quietly become commitments; grant reports with long lead times that feel far away until they aren't; volunteer and partner threads that go quiet.

---

## B. Professional practice

Law, therapy and counseling, accounting, medical, architecture, design studio, coaching, independent consulting.

**Nouns:** clients, matters (or cases, engagements, patients — use the word *they* used), referral sources, waitlist, intake
Not: deals, accounts, leads

**Ladder:** inquiry → consult or intake → engaged → active matter → billing → closed → referral back

**Money looks like:** retainers, hourly billing, unbilled time sitting on the clock, accounts receivable, collections

**Who matters:** active clients, referral sources (often the single most under-managed relationship in this kind of business), counterparties, the waitlist

**Extra rule — dated:** anything with a **statutory, filing or court deadline** goes to Band A.
**Extra rule — chronic:** **unbilled or uncollected work** is never dropped from the map. It's invisible in an inbox and it's where the money actually leaks — but it ranks normally, and it does not outrank a filing deadline.

**Confidentiality:** this profile covers professions that hold privileged or clinical material. The deep read passes over whatever is in the mailbox and cannot distinguish it. If they raise it, don't reassure and don't argue — say plainly what happens (it reads their mail, it stays in their account and workspace, the only thing that leaves goes to their own address), say it's their call to make, and let them make it. Never talk someone past this.

**Watch for:** referral sources who haven't heard from them in months; intake inquiries that never got a reply; matters that have gone quiet but were never formally closed.

---

## C. Agency / consultancy / studio

**Nouns:** prospects, proposals, projects, retainers, clients, contractors
**Ladder:** lead → discovery call → proposal or scope out → verbal yes → active project → delivery → review → retainer or expansion

**Money looks like:** proposals outstanding, deposits, milestone invoices, monthly retainers, scope creep that hasn't been repriced

**Who matters:** live prospects, active clients, referral partners, subcontractors and freelancers

**Extra rule — chronic:** **a proposal outstanding more than five days with no reply is a decaying asset.** Never drop it, and let the tiebreakers carry it — an outstanding proposal usually trips several at once. Also flag any active project where the last client contact is older than the last internal work — that's a client who doesn't know what they're paying for.

**Watch for:** scope that grew without a conversation; contractors waiting on a decision; the gap between "we should follow up" and having actually followed up.

---

## D. Product, retail, hospitality, events

Shops, restaurants, e-commerce, event producers, venues, makers.

**Nouns:** orders, bookings, inventory, vendors, venue, staff, customers
**Ladder:** inquiry → quote or hold → booked with deposit → in production or fulfillment → delivered → repeat or review

**Money looks like:** deposits held, balances due, outstanding invoices, vendor payables, inventory committed but not sold

**Who matters:** booked customers with a date coming, vendors and suppliers, the venue or landlord, staff and scheduling

**Extra rule — dated:** **a fixed date that cannot move goes to Band A** — an event date, a ship date, a service date — ordered by date. Anything within 14 days of one with an open dependency is the top of the map.

**Watch for:** a booked date with a missing deposit, permit, vendor confirmation or staffing plan; a supplier who hasn't confirmed; a review or reorder window closing.

---

## E. Other or mixed

Use when nothing above fits, or when two fit equally.

Derive the nouns from how they actually talk. In the interview, listen for the word they use for a unit of work and the word they use for the person who pays — then use exactly those words everywhere, including in the morning brief. Write both into `Business Context.md` under **Vocabulary** so every future session inherits them.

Use the universal ladder: `inquiry → in conversation → committed → in progress → delivered → follow-through`.

**No extra rule.** Band A is whatever hard dates you find; the five universal tiebreakers carry the rest.

---

## How the extra rules apply

Each profile's extra rule is one of two kinds, and they behave differently:

- **Dated** — a filing deadline, a report due, a court date, an event date. These go into **Band A** of the ranking, ordered by date, above everything else. A hard date six days out with an unresolved dependency is the top of the map.
- **Chronic** — uncollected work, unbilled time, a referral source gone quiet, scope that grew without a conversation. These are **guaranteed a place on the map** and must never be silently dropped, but they rank by the universal ladder like anything else. A chronic condition is not an emergency just because it's important, and promoting it above a hard deadline produces a map no operator believes.

---

## The universal priority ladder

Used to order **Band B** — everything without an immovable date. An item can hit several; the more it hits, the higher it ranks.

1. **Money or funding is on the table.** A number has been named; a proposal, invoice, grant decision, or renewal is outstanding.
2. **The ball is in their court.** Someone asked and they haven't answered.
3. **A meeting is within 48 hours, or one happened in the last 48 hours with nothing done since.**
4. **A commitment they made has come due, or comes due this week.**
5. **Time decay.** A live thread going quiet — roughly five or more days since the last touch, at a late stage.

Anything hitting none of these is not today's priority, however important it feels. Say so plainly rather than padding the list.
